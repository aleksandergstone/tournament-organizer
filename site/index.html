Run your tournament on your own computer.

Tournament Organizer is a free desktop app for people who organise cups, leagues and club nights: create the bracket, enter results as they happen and print final standings — offline, without an account, on your own machine.

Why organizers use it

Brackets that stay correct

Change a result and the bracket recomputes: winners advance, and results that became impossible are cleared instead of silently kept.

A bracket you can read at a glance

The bracket is drawn as a spider — rounds as columns, lines joining each match to the one it feeds, the champion called out. The same drawing prints.

Fast result entry

Type both scores and press Enter. Walkover, unfinished and interrupted matches each have their own button.

Works with no internet

No sign-in, no server, no sync. It runs on a laptop in a hall with bad wifi.

Your data stays on your device

Projects are stored locally and exported as one portable .top.json file you can copy anywhere.

Undo, autosave, recovery

Undo and redo every change, autosave locally, and re-import a backup file after a crash or a mistake.

Take the result with you

Export standings and matches as CSV, or print a clean summary sheet for the wall.

Tournament formats

Single elimination Double elimination Round robin Swiss Groups + knockout League season Team match event Individual match event Custom schedule

Double elimination includes a winners bracket, a losers bracket, a grand final and the conditional final reset. Groups + knockout seeds the knockout stage from the group tables you choose. Leagues can be played home & away. Swiss rounds are generated on demand and paired by current points.

Run the event, not just the bracket

Display mode

A big read-only screen for a projector or TV: current match, what is next, the top five and a live clock. It updates by itself and shows no controls.

QR check-in — coming soon

Codes per participant, match and station are being reworked, so the QR screen is hidden for now. Codes already printed keep working; nothing is sent anywhere.

Courts and time slots

Put matches on courts, tables or stations with start times and durations. Double-booking is reported, and a planner fills the day without overlaps.

Group → knockout, previewed

See who advances, in which seeding order, and the bracket that will be created — before you confirm it. Group results are always kept.

Sync across the hall

Share the project on your own Wi-Fi so a referee device or a display computer stays in step. No account, no cloud; newest edit wins, merges are undoable.

Four languages

English, Polish, German and Spanish — switch in Settings. The documents follow it: headings, column names, status words and dates.

Your first tournament takes five steps

  • Create a tournament — name, sport and format.
  • Add participants — one by one, or paste a whole list.
  • Set rules and generate — points, tiebreaks, seeding; the schedule appears instantly.
  • Enter results — as they happen; brackets and standings update by themselves.
  • Export or print — .top.json backup, CSV, or a print sheet.

Questions

Does it need an internet connection?
No. The app is a desktop program; everything runs on your computer. Internet is only needed to download the installer in the first place.

Do I need an account?
No account, no email, no subscription. Install, use it, done.

Where is my data?
In the app's local storage on this computer. Use Output → Save project file… to keep a portable backup you control — that file can be opened on another computer.

What if I enter a result wrongly?
Press Ctrl+Z to undo, or reopen the match and change the score — the bracket recalculates and clears results that no longer make sense.

How many participants can I use?
Bracket formats are tested from 2 up to 64 participants. Leagues and round robins scale further, but keep in mind the number of matches grows quickly.

Is it really free?
Yes — MIT licensed, free for any use. Optional paid extras are not implemented and the core workflow will stay free.

Privacy

Tournament Organizer has no analytics, no telemetry and no account, and it never contacts a server of ours. Your tournaments never leave your computer unless you export them yourself, or you switch on local-network sharing to hand the project to your own referee or display devices on your Wi-Fi. Deleting a project removes it from this device; keeping a backup is your choice.
